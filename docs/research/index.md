# Research

My research sits at the intersection of urban physics, computational fluid dynamics, and environmental sustainability. I develop and apply high-fidelity CFD models to investigate the physical processes that govern urban microclimates, with the aim of supporting evidence-based urban design.

## Research Interests

**Urban Microclimate & Thermal Comfort**
Understanding how urban morphology — building layouts, street geometry, vegetation — governs local temperature, wind speed, and human thermal comfort. I combine CFD simulations with thermal comfort indices to evaluate outdoor spaces under realistic meteorological conditions.

**Wind Flow in Urban Environments**
Modelling wind flow around buildings, including the complex aerodynamics of lift-up structures and street canyons. I use porous media approaches and high-resolution CFD to capture flow features relevant to ventilation and pollutant dispersion.

**Urban Vegetation & Cooling**
Quantifying the cooling potential of trees and green infrastructure in city neighbourhoods. My work addresses both the direct shading effect and the indirect evapotranspiration cooling, across different climate contexts including temperate and tropical hot-humid cities.

**Data-Driven Urban Climate Analysis**
Applying clustering and machine learning techniques to CFD-derived time-series data to identify and characterize urban microclimate zones — enabling scalable analysis of large, heterogeneous urban domains.
